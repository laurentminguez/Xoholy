## What XOholy is

XOholy is a social platform where the whole feed is Christian. Not a Christian *section*
inside a general platform, and not a general platform with a filter — a place where
Christian faith is the shared assumption of everyone posting and everyone watching.

That means the content is as varied as Christian life actually is: teaching and worship,
but also comedy, sport, business, family, music, travel, and ordinary days. **Christianity
is not a content category here. It is the worldview of the community.** A video of a
surfer at sunrise belongs on XOholy for the same reason a sermon does.

## The standard

**A post belongs on XOholy if it is made by, for, or about Christians living the
Christian faith, and nothing in it contradicts the historic Christian creeds.**

The doctrinal floor is the **Apostles' Creed** and the **Nicene Creed** — the confessions
Christians have held in common across nearly two thousand years and across almost every
tradition. Deliberately, that is a floor and not a ceiling.

Under it, all of these are fully welcome, with no hierarchy between them:

Catholic · Orthodox · Anglican · Lutheran · Reformed · Baptist · Methodist ·
Pentecostal · Charismatic · non-denominational · Messianic Jewish

A creedal floor is the broadest defensible line. It admits roughly the whole of global
Christianity while still being specific enough for a moderation system to apply
consistently. Narrower standards — an evangelical statement of faith, say — would exclude
Catholic and Orthodox believers, who together are most of the Christians alive.

### What XOholy does not decide

The platform does not adjudicate disagreements *between* Christians. It has no position
on baptism, church governance, spiritual gifts, eschatology, worship style, Bible
translation, or any of the other questions Christians have discussed in good faith for
centuries.

Instead, posts carry **tradition tags**, and users tune their own feed. If you want more
Reformed teaching and less charismatic worship, that is your dial to turn — not a
judgement XOholy makes on your behalf, and not one it makes on the creator's behalf
either.

This is a deliberate limit on the platform's authority. XOholy moderates the boundary of
the Christian faith; it does not police its interior.

## What is not allowed

### Outside the faith

- Content promoting a **non-Christian religion** as true.
- Content from **non-Trinitarian movements** that self-identify as Christian but deny the
  creeds — the deity of Christ, the Trinity, or the bodily resurrection. This is a
  doctrinal boundary, not a judgement about any individual's sincerity or worth.
  **[REVIEW]** — the reviewer should confirm how this applies to specific movements, and
  the resulting list should be explicit rather than left to a classifier's judgement.
- **Atheist or anti-Christian argument.** Apologetics that *quotes* an objection in order
  to answer it is welcome; the objection presented approvingly is not.
- Content with no relationship to Christian life at all — reposted general-interest
  material with a verse captioned onto it.

### Inside the faith, still not allowed

- **Attacks on other Christian traditions.** Explaining why you hold your convictions is
  welcome. Contempt for believers who hold others is not.
- **Party politics.** XOholy is not a political platform and takes no partisan side.
  Christians disagree politically in good conscience, and a feed that sorts believers by
  party is a feed that has stopped being about Christ. Teaching that touches on moral and
  civic questions is welcome; campaigning is not. **[REVIEW]** — this line is genuinely
  hard to draw and needs a reviewer's judgement, with worked examples.
- **Extreme prosperity teaching** that conditions God's favour on giving, or presents
  faith as a mechanism for guaranteed wealth or healing. **[REVIEW]** — the boundary
  between teaching on generosity and exploitative fundraising needs a reviewer to define
  precisely.
- **Impersonation** of a pastor, ministry, or church.
- **AI-generated content presented as a real person's teaching.** Synthetic sermons in a
  living pastor's voice are prohibited outright. AI used openly as a creative tool is
  fine; AI used to put words in a real person's mouth is not.
- **Solicitation and financial exploitation** — untraceable donation requests, miracle
  claims tied to payment, prayer sold as a product.

## Safety

Safety is judged **separately from doctrine and before it**. No doctrinal merit makes
unsafe content acceptable, and a post is never approved on the grounds that its message
is sound.

Prohibited without exception: sexual content; child sexual abuse material; graphic
violence; content promoting self-harm or suicide; harassment and targeted abuse; content
endangering a minor; spam and malware.

**Child safety carries a legal obligation, not a policy one.** Suspected child sexual
abuse material is reported to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children as
required by 18 U.S.C. § 2258A. This is mandatory, immediate, not appealable, and not a
judgement call.

## Copyright

**Share what moves you. Do not upload what is not yours.**

When you share a link from YouTube, TikTok, or Instagram, XOholy stores the link and
plays the video in that platform's own player. The creator keeps the view, the
watermark, and the attribution. Nothing is copied.

Uploading someone else's video as your own is different, and it is not allowed.

## How moderation works

Every post passes two independent checks before it can appear.

1. **Safety.** An automated classifier screens for the prohibitions above. This runs
   first and its result is final.
2. **Doctrine.** A second classifier assesses the post against this document and returns
   a decision, a confidence score, tradition and topic tags, and a written reason.

From there:

- **Clear pass** → published.
- **Clear fail** → rejected, with the reason shown to the creator and an appeal offered.
- **Anything uncertain** → a person reviews it. Machines do not get to make the close
  calls about someone's faith.

A first-time poster is always reviewed by a human. Creators with a track record of
approved posts and no upheld reports are sampled rather than screened. If a published
post starts attracting reports, it is automatically unpublished pending re-review.

### One honest limitation

For content **shared from another platform**, XOholy can only see the title, description,
thumbnail, and creator — not the audio. Listening to it would mean downloading someone
else's video, which is exactly what this platform does not do.

So doctrinal review is genuinely stronger on native uploads than on shares. XOholy
compensates with creator reputation, mandatory human review of first-time sharers, and a
low report threshold — but it is better for you to know this than for the platform to
imply a certainty it does not have.

## Appeals

Every rejection can be appealed, and every appeal is read by a person.

You will always be told **why** — the reason your post was rejected is recorded when the
decision is made, precisely so it can be quoted back to you. A decision that cannot be
explained is not a decision anyone should have to accept.

If we got it wrong, we restore the post and correct the standard that produced the error.

## Reporting

Every post can be reported from the post itself. Reports are read. Reporting content
that is genuinely outside these standards helps far more than commenting about it — and
you can block any account, which hides your content from them as well as theirs from you.

## For the reviewer

If you are the pastor or theologian reviewing this document, these are the decisions we
most need from you. We have marked our best attempt, not our conclusion.

1. **The creedal floor.** Are the Apostles' and Nicene Creeds the right standard, and is
   there a more precise formulation you would prefer?
2. **Non-Trinitarian movements.** Which specific movements does the boundary exclude, and
   how should that be worded so it is clear without being contemptuous?
3. **Politics.** Where does teaching on moral and civic questions end and partisanship
   begin? Worked examples would be more useful to us than a principle.
4. **Prosperity teaching.** Where is the line between teaching on generosity and
   exploitation?
5. **The tradition tags.** Is the list complete, and are the labels ones each tradition
   would recognise as fair?
6. **Tone.** These standards will be read by people who have been turned away. Does this
   document sound like the church at its best?
